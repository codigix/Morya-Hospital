Introduction

Cataracts are one of the most common causes of vision problems, particularly as people get older. A cataract develops when the normally clear lens inside the eye becomes cloudy, causing blurred or hazy vision. It can gradually affect reading, driving, recognising faces and performing everyday activities. For patients looking for Cataract Surgery in PCMC, understanding the symptoms, diagnosis, treatment options and recovery process can help them make informed decisions about their eye health.
At Moraya Multispeciality Charitable Hospital , Chinchwad, patients can seek ophthalmology consultation and appropriate evaluation for cataract-related vision problems. Early assessment by an eye specialist can help determine whether cataract treatment or surgery is appropriate.

What Is a Cataract?

A cataract occurs when the natural lens of the eye becomes cloudy instead of remaining clear. Because the lens helps focus light onto the retina, clouding can interfere with normal vision.
Cataracts commonly develop with ageing, but they may also occur due to diabetes, previous eye injuries, certain medications, prolonged exposure to ultraviolet radiation or other eye conditions. Cataracts may affect one or both eyes, although the severity may differ between the two.
A cataract does not usually cause sudden vision loss. Instead, symptoms often develop gradually and may become more noticeable over time.

Common Symptoms of Cataracts

Recognising cataract symptoms early can encourage patients to seek an ophthalmology consultation before vision problems significantly interfere with daily activities.
Common symptoms include:

  • ✔ Blurred or cloudy vision
  • ✔ Difficulty seeing clearly at night
  • ✔ Increased sensitivity to bright lights
  • ✔ Glare around headlights and lamps
  • ✔ Halos around lights
  • ✔ Difficulty reading despite adequate lighting
  • ✔ Colours appearing faded or less vivid
  • ✔ Frequent changes in spectacle prescription
  • ✔ Difficulty recognising faces from a distance
  • ✔ Reduced ability to drive comfortably, particularly at night

Some people initially manage their symptoms by using brighter lights or changing their glasses. However, if vision continues to deteriorate, an eye examination is important.

When Should You Consult an Ophthalmologist?

Not every change in vision is caused by cataracts. Blurred vision can also occur because of refractive errors, glaucoma, diabetic eye disease, retinal conditions or other eye problems.
You should consider an ophthalmology consultation if:

  • ✔ Your vision is becoming progressively blurred.
  • ✔ You experience difficulty reading or doing routine activities.
  • ✔ Night driving has become uncomfortable or unsafe.
  • ✔ Bright lights cause significant glare.
  • ✔ Your spectacle prescription changes frequently.
  • ✔ Cataract symptoms are interfering with work or daily life.
  • ✔ You have diabetes or another condition that may affect eye health.

An ophthalmologist can examine the eyes and determine the underlying cause of your vision changes.

How Is a Cataract Diagnosed?

Cataract diagnosis generally involves a comprehensive eye examination. The ophthalmologist may assess visual acuity and examine the different structures of the eye.
Depending on the patient's condition, evaluation may include:
Visual Acuity Test
This checks how clearly a patient can see at different distances. It helps assess the degree to which vision is affected.
Slit-Lamp Examination
A slit lamp allows the ophthalmologist to examine the front structures of the eye, including the cornea, iris and lens. It can help identify the presence and severity of a cataract.
Dilated Eye Examination
Eye drops may be used to widen the pupil so that the ophthalmologist can examine the retina and optic nerve. This is important because other eye conditions may exist alongside a cataract.
Additional Measurements Before Surgery
If cataract surgery is recommended, measurements of the eye may be performed to help determine the appropriate intraocular lens (IOL) and surgical plan.
Cataract Treatment Options
The appropriate treatment depends on the severity of the cataract and how much it affects the patient's vision and daily activities.
Updated Spectacles and Better Lighting
In the early stages, stronger lighting, anti-glare measures or an updated spectacle prescription may temporarily improve visual function. These measures, however, do not remove the cataract.
Cataract Surgery
When the cataract significantly affects vision or quality of life, cataract surgery may be recommended. Surgery involves removing the cloudy natural lens and replacing it with an artificial intraocular lens.
Modern cataract surgery is generally performed using small-incision techniques, with the exact procedure depending on the patient's eye health and the surgeon's assessment.

What Are the Different Cataract Surgery Options?

Cataract surgery is not necessarily the same for every patient. The ophthalmologist considers factors such as age, eye health, lifestyle, existing eye conditions and visual requirements when planning treatment.
Phacoemulsification Phacoemulsification is a commonly used cataract surgery technique. A small incision is made in the eye, and ultrasound energy is used to break the cloudy lens into smaller pieces. The lens fragments are then removed, and an intraocular lens is placed inside the eye. Intraocular Lens Options An IOL replaces the eye's natural cloudy lens. Different lens options may be available depending on the patient's requirements.
The ophthalmologist may discuss options such as:

  • ✔ Monofocal lenses – generally designed to provide clear vision at one primary distance.
  • ✔ Multifocal or advanced-technology lenses – may provide vision at more than one distance for selected patients.
  • ✔ Toric lenses – may be considered for patients with significant astigmatism.

The most appropriate IOL depends on the patient's eye measurements, visual needs and overall eye health. Patients should discuss the benefits, limitations and potential costs of each option with their ophthalmologist.

What Happens During Cataract Surgery?

Before surgery, the patient undergoes an eye examination and measurements required for surgical planning. The procedure is commonly performed using local or topical anaesthesia, depending on the clinical situation.
During surgery, the cloudy lens is removed and replaced with an artificial intraocular lens. The procedure is usually completed within a relatively short period, although the exact duration can vary depending on the patient's condition and surgical requirements.
After surgery, patients are generally monitored for a period before being allowed to return home according to their doctor's advice.

Cataract Surgery Recovery

Recovery after cataract surgery varies between individuals. Vision may initially be slightly blurred or the eye may feel mildly uncomfortable. Patients should follow all postoperative instructions provided by their ophthalmologist.
Typical postoperative advice may include:

  • ✔ Use prescribed eye drops as directed.
  • ✔ Avoid rubbing or pressing the operated eye.
  • ✔ Keep the eye protected as advised.
  • ✔ Attend scheduled follow-up examinations.
  • ✔ Avoid strenuous activity until the ophthalmologist says it is safe.
  • ✔ Follow instructions regarding bathing, swimming and other activities.

Many patients notice an improvement in vision relatively soon after surgery, but complete visual stabilization can take longer.

What Should Patients Know Before Cataract Surgery?

Choosing cataract surgery should be based on the patient's symptoms, eye examination, and lifestyle rather than age alone. A cataract does not always need to be removed immediately after diagnosis.
Patients should discuss:

  • ✔ How much the cataract is affecting daily activities
  • ✔ The recommended surgical technique
  • ✔ Available IOL options
  • ✔ Expected visual outcomes
  • ✔ Existing eye conditions
  • ✔ Postoperative care
  • ✔ Follow-up requirements
  • ✔ Possible risks and complications

It is also important to tell the ophthalmologist about existing medical conditions, medications, and previous eye surgeries.

When Is Cataract Surgery Usually Recommended?

Cataract surgery may be considered when the cataract causes enough visual impairment to interfere with activities such as reading, working, driving, watching television, or moving around
The decision is made after an ophthalmology evaluation. A patient does not necessarily need to wait until vision becomes severely impaired before discussing surgery.

Conclusion

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick information to help patients understand our services and facilities.

What are the common symptoms of cataracts?

Common cataract symptoms include blurred or cloudy vision, difficulty seeing at night, glare from headlights or bright lights, halos around lights, faded colours and frequent changes in spectacle prescription. Symptoms usually develop gradually.

How is a cataract diagnosed?

An ophthalmologist diagnoses a cataract through a comprehensive eye examination. This may include a visual acuity test, slit-lamp examination and a dilated eye examination. Additional eye measurements may be required if surgery is being considered.

Do all cataracts require surgery?

No. Cataracts do not always require immediate surgery. In the early stages, updated spectacles and better lighting may help manage some vision difficulties. Surgery is generally considered when the cataract begins to interfere significantly with daily activities.

What happens during cataract surgery?

During cataract surgery, the cloudy natural lens is removed and replaced with an artificial intraocular lens (IOL). A commonly used technique is phacoemulsification, in which ultrasound energy helps break up and remove the cloudy lens through a small incision.
