Common Ear, Nose, and Throat Problems

ENT conditions can affect the ears, nose, sinuses, throat, and voice box. Some of the common problems include

1. Ear Infections

Ear infections are common in children but can also affect adults. Symptoms may include ear pain, blocked ears, discharge, fever, and temporary hearing difficulty. Recurrent infections should be evaluated because repeated inflammation may affect ear health and hearing.
An ENT specialist can examine the ear canal and eardrum, identify the possible cause, and recommend suitable treatment. Depending on the condition, treatment may include prescribed medicines, ear care, monitoring, or further intervention.

2. Hearing Loss

Hearing loss can develop gradually or occur suddenly. Age-related changes, ear infections, prolonged exposure to loud sounds, congenital conditions, and other medical problems may contribute to hearing impairment.
Common signs include difficulty understanding conversations, increasing the television volume, frequently asking others to repeat themselves, difficulty hearing in crowded places, ringing in the ears, and speech or hearing concerns in children.
Early assessment can help determine the type and degree of hearing loss. Depending on the condition, treatment may include medical management, hearing aids, hearing rehabilitation, or evaluation for advanced options such as cochlear implantation.

3. Sinusitis

Sinusitis occurs when the tissues lining the sinuses become inflamed. Symptoms may include nasal blockage, facial pressure, headache, nasal discharge, reduced sense of smell, and post-nasal drip.
An ENT specialist in PCMC can evaluate whether symptoms are associated with infection, allergies, nasal structural problems, or chronic sinus disease. Treatment may include prescribed medicines, nasal therapy, allergy management, and, in selected cases, surgical treatment.

4. Allergic Rhinitis

Allergic rhinitis can cause repeated sneezing, a runny or blocked nose, itchy eyes, and nasal irritation. Dust, pollen, pollution, and other environmental allergens may trigger symptoms.
Treatment focuses on controlling symptoms and reducing exposure to known triggers. Depending on the patient's condition, an ENT specialist may recommend appropriate medicines, nasal treatment, and allergy-management strategies.

5. Tonsillitis and Throat Infections

Tonsillitis and throat infections may cause sore throat, difficulty swallowing, fever, swollen tonsils and general discomfort. Recurrent infections can affect school, work, sleep and daily activities.
An ENT consultation can help determine the cause of recurring symptoms and whether medical treatment, monitoring, or further evaluation is required.

6. Snoring and Sleep-Related Breathing Problems

Regular or loud snoring may sometimes be associated with nasal blockage, enlarged tonsils or adenoids, airway problems or sleep-related breathing disorders.
Loud snoring, pauses in breathing during sleep, restless sleep, morning headaches or excessive daytime sleepiness should be evaluated. An ENT specialist can examine the upper airway and recommend appropriate management.

7. Voice and Throat Problems

Persistent hoarseness, voice changes, throat discomfort, or difficulty swallowing should be evaluated when symptoms continue or recur. An ENT examination can help identify possible problems involving the throat or voice box and determine whether further testing or treatment is required.

8. Dizziness and Balance Problems

Dizziness or vertigo can sometimes be related to problems affecting the inner ear. Patients may experience spinning sensations, imbalance, nausea or difficulty walking.
Because dizziness can have several causes, proper evaluation is important to determine whether the symptoms are related to the ear or another medical condition.

When Should You Consult an ENT Specialist?

You should consider an ENT consultation when symptoms are persistent, recurrent, worsening or affecting your daily life.
Seek specialist evaluation for:

  • ✔ Persistent ear pain or discharge
  • ✔ Hearing loss or sudden hearing changes
  • ✔ Recurrent ear infections
  • ✔ Persistent ringing or buzzing in the ears
  • ✔ Repeated sinus infections
  • ✔ Long-lasting nasal blockage
  • ✔ Difficulty breathing through the nose
  • ✔ Frequent throat infections
  • ✔ Persistent hoarseness
  • ✔ Difficulty swallowing
  • ✔ Recurrent dizziness or vertigo
  • ✔ Loud or persistent snoring
  • ✔ Speech or hearing concerns in children

Sudden hearing loss, severe breathing difficulty, significant bleeding or other acute symptoms require prompt medical attention.

ENT Diagnosis: What Tests May Be Required?

. Ear Examination: The ear canal and eardrum may be examined to identify infection, wax buildup, inflammation, or other abnormalities.
Hearing Tests: Audiological evaluation can help determine the type and degree of hearing loss. The test selected may depend on the patient's age and symptoms.
Nasal and Sinus Evaluation: The nasal passages may be examined to identify inflammation, infection, polyps, structural abnormalities, or other causes of nasal obstruction.
Throat and Voice Examination: Patients with persistent throat or voice symptoms may require detailed examination of the throat and voice box.
Imaging: In selected cases, imaging such as CT scans may be recommended to assess the sinuses or other ENT structures in greater detail.
ENT Treatment Options Treatment depends on the underlying cause and the patient's individual condition.
Medicines: Depending on the diagnosis, prescribed medicines may be used for appropriate infections, allergies, nasal symptoms, pain, or other conditions.
Hearing Aids: For certain types of permanent hearing loss, hearing aids may help improve hearing and communication.
Cochlear Implant Evaluation: Patients with severe to profound hearing loss who receive limited benefit from conventional hearing aids may be evaluated for cochlear implantation. Children with significant hearing loss may also benefit from early assessment and hearing rehabilitation.
Nasal and Sinus Treatment: Allergy management, nasal medicines, and other conservative approaches may help many patients. Selected patients with structural or chronic sinus problems may require surgical treatment.
Tonsil or Adenoid Surgery: Surgery may be considered for patients with recurrent tonsillitis or significantly enlarged tonsils or adenoids causing breathing or sleep-related problems, following specialist evaluation.
ENT Surgery: Certain ear, nose, sinus, and throat conditions may require minimally invasive or conventional surgery when conservative treatment is not sufficient.

You should consider an ENT consultation for persistent or recurrent ear infections, hearing loss, sinus problems, nasal blockage, throat infections, dizziness, snoring, swallowing difficulties, or ongoing voice changes.

What tests can an ENT specialist perform?
