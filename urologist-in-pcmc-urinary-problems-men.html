Introduction

Urinary problems can affect men at different stages of life. Some symptoms may be temporary and related to an infection or lifestyle factors, while others can be associated with conditions involving the bladder, kidneys, prostate or urinary tract. Symptoms such as frequent urination, burning while passing urine, difficulty starting urination, a weak urine stream or blood in the urine should not be ignored when they persist or recur.
For men looking for a Urologist in PCMC, understanding common urinary symptoms and knowing when to seek medical evaluation can help with timely diagnosis and appropriate treatment. A urologist specialises in conditions affecting the urinary system in men and women, as well as the male reproductive system.
At Moraya Multispeciality Charitable Hospital , Chinchwad, patients can access urology and nephrology services as part of its multispeciality healthcare facilities.

What Does a Urologist Treat?

A urologist diagnoses and treats conditions involving the urinary tract and, in men, the male reproductive system. The urinary system includes the kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra.
Men may consult a urologist for conditions such as:

  • ✔ Urinary tract infections
  • ✔ Kidney and urinary stones
  • ✔ Enlarged prostate
  • ✔ Difficulty passing urine
  • ✔ Urinary incontinence
  • ✔ Blood in the urine
  • ✔ Recurrent urinary problems
  • ✔ Bladder conditions
  • ✔ Certain kidney and urinary tract conditions
  • ✔ Male reproductive and sexual health conditions

The appropriate treatment depends on the cause of the symptoms, the patient's age, medical history and diagnostic findings.

Common Urinary Problems in Men

Urinary symptoms can vary considerably. Some men may experience one symptom, while others may have several symptoms at the same time.
Frequent Urination
Needing to urinate more often than usual can have several possible causes. These may include increased fluid intake, urinary infection, diabetes, bladder conditions or prostate-related problems.
If frequent urination is persistent, occurs at night repeatedly or is accompanied by other symptoms, a medical evaluation may be appropriate.
Burning or Pain While Urinating
A burning sensation during urination can occur with urinary tract infections and other urinary conditions.
Men experiencing persistent burning, pain, fever, discharge or other associated symptoms should consult a healthcare professional for assessment rather than assuming the cause.
Weak Urine Stream
A weak or slow urine stream can occur when urine flow is restricted. In older men, an enlarged prostate is one possible cause.
A urologist can assess the symptoms and determine whether further investigations are required.
Difficulty Starting Urination
Some men may find it difficult to begin urinating or may need to strain to start the flow. This symptom can be associated with prostate or urinary tract problems and should be evaluated if it persists.
Urgency to Urinate
A sudden and difficult-to-control urge to urinate can interfere with daily activities and sleep. Persistent urinary urgency may have different causes and should be assessed according to the individual's symptoms.
Getting Up Frequently at Night
Repeatedly waking during the night to urinate is known as nocturia. Occasional nighttime urination may not indicate a serious problem, but frequent or worsening nocturia can affect sleep and quality of life.
A doctor can assess possible contributing factors, including urinary, prostate, metabolic or lifestyle-related causes.

Enlarged Prostate and Urinary Symptoms

Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), commonly known as an enlarged prostate, becomes more common as men age. It can cause lower urinary tract symptoms because an enlarged prostate can affect urine flow from the bladder.
Possible symptoms include:

  • ✔ Difficulty starting urination
  • ✔ Weak or interrupted urine stream
  • ✔ Dribbling at the end of urination
  • ✔ Frequent urination
  • ✔ Increased nighttime urination
  • ✔ Urinary urgency
  • ✔ Feeling that the bladder has not emptied completely

Not every urinary symptom in an older man is caused by an enlarged prostate. A urologist can evaluate the symptoms and determine the appropriate next steps.

Urinary Tract Infections in Men

Urinary tract infections can occur in men, although they are less common than in women. Symptoms can include burning while urinating, increased urinary frequency, urgency and discomfort.
Some patients may also develop fever or other systemic symptoms.
Men with suspected urinary infections should receive appropriate medical evaluation. A healthcare professional may recommend urine testing and other investigations depending on the symptoms.
Treatment should be based on medical assessment rather than self-medication.

Kidney and Urinary Stones

Kidney stones can cause significant pain when they move through the urinary tract. Depending on their location and size, stones may cause pain in the side, back or lower abdomen and may be associated with blood in the urine, nausea or urinary symptoms.
Some small stones may pass naturally, while larger or obstructing stones may require medical or surgical treatment.
A urologist can assess the stone's size, location and other clinical factors and recommend an appropriate treatment approach.

Blood in Urine Should Not Be Ignored

Blood in the urine, known medically as haematuria, can have several causes. These may include urinary infections, stones, prostate conditions and other urinary tract problems.
Even when there is no pain, visible blood in the urine should be medically evaluated.
The doctor may recommend urine tests, imaging or other investigations to determine the cause.

Urinary Incontinence in Men

Urinary incontinence refers to involuntary leakage of urine. It can occur for different reasons, including prostate-related problems, neurological conditions, bladder problems or after certain medical procedures.
Although urinary leakage can feel embarrassing, it is a medical symptom that can often be evaluated and managed.
Men experiencing persistent urine leakage should discuss the problem with a healthcare professional.

When Should a Man Consult a Urologist?

You may consider consulting a Urologist in PCMC if urinary symptoms are persistent, recurrent, worsening or affecting your daily life.
A urological evaluation may be appropriate for symptoms such as:

  • ✔ Persistent difficulty passing urine
  • ✔ Weak or interrupted urine flow
  • ✔ Recurrent urinary infections
  • ✔ Blood in the urine
  • ✔ Frequent nighttime urination
  • ✔ Persistent urinary urgency
  • ✔ Pain while urinating
  • ✔ Recurrent kidney or urinary stones
  • ✔ Incomplete bladder emptying
  • ✔ Persistent pelvic or urinary discomfort

Early medical evaluation can help identify the underlying cause and determine whether treatment or further investigation is required.

When Is Urinary Trouble an Emergency?

Some urinary symptoms require urgent medical attention.
Seek prompt medical care if you experience:

  • ✔ Inability to pass urine despite a strong urge
  • ✔ Severe or worsening pain
  • ✔ Blood in the urine accompanied by concerning symptoms
  • ✔ Fever with urinary symptoms, particularly if you feel seriously unwell
  • ✔ Severe pain associated with suspected kidney stones
  • ✔ Sudden deterioration in your condition

If symptoms appear severe or potentially life-threatening, seek emergency medical care rather than waiting for a routine urology appointment.

What Happens During a Urology Consultation?

A urology consultation generally begins with a discussion of symptoms and medical history. The doctor may ask about:

  • ✔ When symptoms started
  • ✔ How frequently they occur
  • ✔ Urination patterns
  • ✔ Previous urinary infections
  • ✔ Previous kidney stones
  • ✔ Existing medical conditions
  • ✔ Medications
  • ✔ Previous surgeries or procedures

Depending on the symptoms, the doctor may recommend investigations such as urine testing, blood tests, ultrasound, imaging or other specialised tests.
For prostate-related urinary symptoms, the doctor may recommend additional evaluation based on the patient's age, symptoms and risk factors.

Tests a Urologist May Recommend

The specific tests required depend on the suspected condition. Possible investigations can include:
Urine Tests
Urine analysis can help identify signs of infection, blood or other abnormalities.
Blood Tests
Blood tests may be used to assess general health or evaluate kidney function and other relevant parameters.
Ultrasound
Ultrasound may be used to examine the kidneys, bladder and, when appropriate, the prostate and urinary tract.
CT or Other Imaging
In selected cases, imaging may be recommended to investigate stones or other urinary tract conditions.
Urine Flow and Bladder Assessment
Depending on the symptoms, a urologist may recommend tests to assess urine flow and bladder emptying.
Patients should undergo investigations based on their doctor's recommendation rather than requesting unnecessary tests.

Treatment for Urinary Problems

Treatment depends entirely on the underlying cause.
Depending on the diagnosis, management may include:

  • ✔ Lifestyle modifications
  • ✔ Medication
  • ✔ Treatment for urinary infection
  • ✔ Management of prostate-related symptoms
  • ✔ Treatment for urinary stones
  • ✔ Bladder training or other supportive measures
  • ✔ Physiotherapy in selected cases
  • ✔ Minimally invasive procedures
  • ✔ Surgery when clinically indicated

The appropriate treatment should be decided by a qualified healthcare professional after assessment.

Lifestyle Measures for Better Urinary Health

Certain general habits may support urinary health, although they do not replace medical treatment.
Men can discuss the following with their healthcare provider:

  • ✔ Maintain appropriate fluid intake
  • ✔ Avoid excessive alcohol and caffeine if they worsen urinary symptoms
  • ✔ Do not routinely hold urine for prolonged periods
  • ✔ Maintain a healthy body weight
  • ✔ Stay physically active
  • ✔ Manage conditions such as diabetes and high blood pressure
  • ✔ Take prescribed medicines as directed
  • ✔ Seek medical attention for persistent urinary symptoms

Fluid requirements vary between individuals, particularly for people with kidney, heart or other medical conditions, so personalised advice from a healthcare professional is important.

Why Timely Urological Evaluation Matters

Urinary symptoms can have many different causes, and the same symptom can occur in more than one condition. For example, frequent urination may be associated with urinary infection, prostate problems, diabetes or other conditions.
Therefore, it is important not to assume the cause based on symptoms alone.
A urologist can review the patient's history, perform an appropriate examination and recommend investigations when necessary. This helps determine whether the symptoms require medication, monitoring, lifestyle changes, a procedure or another form of treatment.

Conclusion

Urinary problems in men can range from temporary discomfort to conditions requiring specialist treatment. Symptoms such as persistent urinary difficulty, weak urine flow, frequent nighttime urination, recurrent infections, kidney stones or blood in the urine should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick information to help patients understand our services and facilities.

When should men consult a urologist in PCMC?

Men should consider consulting a urologist for persistent or recurring urinary problems such as difficulty passing urine, weak urine flow, frequent nighttime urination, blood in the urine, recurrent infections, urinary urgency or kidney stones.

What are common urinary problems in men?

Common problems include frequent urination, burning while urinating, weak urine flow, difficulty starting urination, urinary urgency, incomplete bladder emptying, urinary incontinence, kidney stones and prostate-related urinary symptoms.

Can an enlarged prostate cause urinary problems in men?

Yes. An enlarged prostate, also known as benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), can contribute to symptoms such as difficulty starting urination, weak or interrupted urine flow, frequent urination, nighttime urination and incomplete bladder emptying.

What tests may a urologist recommend for urinary problems?

Depending on the symptoms, a urologist may recommend urine and blood tests, ultrasound, CT or other imaging, urine-flow testing and bladder assessment. The appropriate tests depend on the patient's individual symptoms and medical history.
