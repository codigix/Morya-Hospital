Hearing problems can affect people at different stages of life, and treatment requirements can vary considerably between children and adults.
For children, hearing is closely connected with speech, language, learning and development. Early identification and appropriate intervention may therefore be particularly important.
Adults with hearing loss may experience difficulty communicating at home, at work, over the telephone or in noisy environments.

Cochlear Implant Care for Children

Children with severe to profound sensorineural hearing loss may be considered for cochlear implantation when they meet appropriate clinical and audiological criteria.
Before recommending treatment, the child's hearing history and overall development need to be carefully assessed.
A paediatric cochlear implant evaluation may include:

  • ✔ Detailed hearing assessment
  • ✔ Audiological evaluation
  • ✔ Hearing-aid benefit assessment
  • ✔ Speech and language assessment
  • ✔ ENT examination
  • ✔ Medical evaluation
  • ✔ Imaging when required
  • ✔ Assessment of cochlear implant candidacy
  • ✔ Parent counselling

Early identification of significant hearing loss can help families explore appropriate intervention options sooner.

Why Early Hearing Care Matters

Children learn to communicate partly through access to sound. When significant hearing loss is identified, timely intervention can help parents and specialists develop an appropriate hearing and communication plan.
Depending on the child's condition, treatment may involve hearing aids, medical or surgical treatment, auditory rehabilitation or cochlear implant evaluation.
A cochlear implant is not automatically recommended for every child with hearing loss. Candidacy is determined after detailed assessment.

Cochlear Implant Care for Adults

Adults with severe hearing loss may have difficulty understanding conversations, participating in meetings, communicating in noisy environments or using telephones. For selected adults with severe to profound sensorineural hearing loss who receive limited benefit from appropriately fitted hearing aids, cochlear implant evaluation may be considered. The assessment can include:

  • ✔ Hearing tests
  • ✔ Speech perception assessment
  • ✔ Hearing-aid benefit evaluation
  • ✔ ENT examination
  • ✔ Medical assessment
  • ✔ Imaging when required
  • ✔ Discussion of treatment expectations
  • ✔ Rehabilitation planning

The objective is to understand the patient's hearing needs and determine whether cochlear implantation or another hearing-management option may be appropriate.

What Is a Cochlear Implant?

A cochlear implant is an electronic hearing device designed for selected people with severe to profound sensorineural hearing loss.
Unlike a conventional hearing aid, which primarily amplifies sound, a cochlear implant converts sound into electrical signals that stimulate the auditory nerve. A cochlear implant generally includes an external sound processor and an internal component that is surgically placed.
The aim is to provide access to sound for appropriately selected patients. However, a cochlear implant does not guarantee normal hearing, and outcomes can vary between individuals.

Who May Need a Cochlear Implant Evaluation?

A cochlear implant assessment may be considered for patients who have:

  • ✔ Severe to profound sensorineural hearing loss
  • ✔ Limited benefit from appropriately fitted hearing aids
  • ✔ Difficulty understanding speech
  • ✔ Significant communication difficulties
  • ✔ Hearing loss affecting education, work or daily activities

For children, additional considerations may include age, duration of hearing loss, hearing history, speech and language development and access to appropriate rehabilitation. A detailed evaluation is essential before making a treatment decision.

The Cochlear Implant Treatment Journey

Cochlear implant treatment involves several stages rather than just the surgical procedure.
1. ENT Consultation
The process begins with an ENT consultation. The specialist reviews the patient's hearing history, symptoms, previous treatment and current communication difficulties.
2. Comprehensive Hearing Assessment
Audiological testing helps determine the type and severity of hearing loss and the amount of benefit obtained from hearing aids.
3. Medical and Imaging Evaluation
Depending on the individual case, medical investigations and imaging may be recommended to assess the anatomy of the ear and cochlear.
4. Candidacy Evaluation
The available test results are reviewed to determine whether cochlear implantation may be appropriate.
5. Treatment Planning
If implantation is considered suitable, the patient and family can discuss the procedure, implant options, expected stages of care and rehabilitation requirements.
6. Cochlear Implant Surgery
When surgery is recommended, the internal implant is placed surgically and the electrode array is positioned within the cochlea.
7. Activation and Programming
After an appropriate healing period, the external sound processor is activated and programmed.
8. Rehabilitation and Follow-Up
Regular audiological follow-up, programming and auditory rehabilitation can be important for adapting to the implant and making effective use of the device.

Why Comprehensive ENT Evaluation Is Important

Hearing loss can have many causes. Some conditions may respond to medical or surgical treatment, while others may require hearing aids or cochlear implant evaluation.
A comprehensive ENT assessment helps determine:

  • ✔ The type of hearing loss
  • ✔ The severity of hearing loss
  • ✔ Whether the hearing loss affects one or both ears
  • ✔ The possible underlying cause
  • ✔ The benefit obtained from hearing aids
  • ✔ Whether further investigations are required
  • ✔ Whether cochlear implantation may be appropriate

This makes specialist evaluation an important first step before choosing a treatment.

Rehabilitation After Cochlear Implant Surgery

One of the most important aspects of cochlear implant care is understanding that surgery is only one stage of treatment. After activation, the brain needs time to adapt to the new auditory signals. Depending on the patient's needs, rehabilitation can include:

  • ✔ Audiology follow-up
  • ✔ Device programming or mapping
  • ✔ Hearing assessments
  • ✔ Auditory training
  • ✔ Listening exercises
  • ✔ Speech and language support
  • ✔ Regular monitoring

The rehabilitation process differs between individuals. Age, duration of hearing loss, previous hearing experience and consistent device use can all influence the overall journey.

Consider an ENT evaluation if you or your child experiences persistent hearing-related concerns such as:

  • ✔ Difficulty hearing conversations
  • ✔ Frequently asking others to repeat themselves
  • ✔ Delayed speech development
  • ✔ Poor response to sounds
  • ✔ Recurrent ear problems
  • ✔ Difficulty understanding speech in noisy environments
  • ✔ Significant or progressive hearing loss
  • ✔ Limited benefit from hearing aids
  • ✔ Hearing difficulties affecting school, work or daily activities

Early evaluation can help identify the underlying problem and determine what treatment options may be appropriate.
