What Does Orthopaedic Treatment Include?

Orthopaedic treatment focuses on conditions affecting the bones, joints, muscles, ligaments, tendons and other parts of the musculoskeletal system. Depending on the condition, treatment may include medication, physiotherapy, lifestyle modifications, injections, fracture management or surgery.
An orthopaedic specialist generally evaluates the patient's symptoms, medical history and physical condition. Diagnostic tests such as X-rays, CT scans or other imaging investigations may be recommended when required.

Common Bone, Joint & Muscle Problems

1. Arthritis and Joint Pain

Arthritis is one of the common causes of persistent joint pain and stiffness. It can affect the knees, hips, shoulders, hands and other joints. Osteoarthritis, for example, may develop gradually as the cartilage within a joint wears down.
Common symptoms include:

  • ✔ Joint pain and stiffness
  • ✔ Swelling around the joint
  • ✔ Difficulty walking or climbing stairs
  • ✔ Reduced range of movement
  • ✔ Pain that worsens with activity

Early assessment can help determine whether conservative treatment, physiotherapy or another intervention may be appropriate.

2. Knee Pain

Knee pain can result from arthritis, injuries, ligament problems, overuse or age-related changes. People who spend long hours standing, participate in sports or have previous knee injuries may also experience recurring knee problems.
Treatment depends on the underlying cause. It may involve rest, medication, physiotherapy, exercises, weight management or surgical treatment in more advanced cases.
If knee pain continues despite basic measures or begins interfering with everyday activities, consulting an orthopaedic specialist is advisable.

3. Fractures and Bone Injuries

Fractures can occur following road accidents, falls, sports injuries or other trauma. Some fractures require immediate medical attention and proper immobilisation to support healing. Depending on the location and severity of the fracture, treatment may include:

  • ✔ Splints or casts
  • ✔ Reduction of the fractured bone
  • ✔ Medication for pain management
  • ✔ Follow-up imaging
  • ✔ Surgical fixation when required
  • ✔ Physiotherapy during recovery

Prompt evaluation is particularly important after significant trauma, especially when there is severe pain, swelling, deformity or difficulty moving the affected limb.

4. Back and Neck Pain

Back and neck pain are increasingly common among people who spend extended periods sitting at desks or using computers and mobile devices. Poor posture, muscle strain, injuries and certain spinal conditions can contribute to discomfort.
Symptoms may include pain, stiffness, restricted movement or pain radiating towards the arms or legs.
Not every case of back or neck pain requires surgery. An orthopaedic assessment can help identify the likely cause and determine an appropriate treatment plan.

5. Shoulder Problems

Shoulder pain can make routine activities such as lifting objects, reaching overhead or getting dressed difficult. Common causes include muscle strain, tendon problems, sports injuries, frozen shoulder and age-related changes.
Depending on the diagnosis, treatment may include medication, physiotherapy, exercises, activity modification or surgery when necessary.

6. Sports Injuries

Sports and physical activities can sometimes result in sprains, muscle injuries, ligament injuries, dislocations and fractures. Athletes may also develop overuse injuries because of repetitive movements or inadequate recovery.
Proper diagnosis is important because returning to activity too quickly can increase the risk of reinjury. Rehabilitation and physiotherapy can play an important role in restoring strength, flexibility and movement.

8. Muscle and Soft-Tissue Injuries

Muscle strains and soft-tissue injuries may occur during exercise, lifting, work-related activities or sudden movements. Mild injuries may improve with rest and appropriate rehabilitation, while severe injuries may require specialist assessment.
Persistent swelling, weakness or pain should be evaluated rather than repeatedly treated with home remedies.

When Should You See an Orthopaedic Specialist?

You should consider an orthopaedic consultation if you experience:

  • ✔ Persistent bone or joint pain
  • ✔ Repeated swelling or stiffness
  • ✔ Difficulty walking or moving a joint
  • ✔ Pain following an accident or fall
  • ✔ Suspected fracture or dislocation
  • ✔ Recurring sports injuries
  • ✔ Reduced strength or range of motion
  • ✔ Knee or hip pain affecting daily activities
  • ✔ Back or neck pain that does not improve
  • ✔ Joint pain that is progressively getting worse

Sudden severe pain, obvious deformity, inability to bear weight or a serious injury may require urgent medical evaluation.

Orthopaedic Treatment Options

Orthopaedic care is not limited to surgery. The treatment recommended depends on the diagnosis, severity of the condition, age, activity level and overall health.
Conservative Treatment
For many musculoskeletal conditions, initial treatment may include medication, rest, activity modification, exercises and physiotherapy.
Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation Physiotherapy can help improve mobility, flexibility, muscle strength and function. It may be recommended following an injury, fracture or surgery or for certain chronic conditions.
Fracture Management Fractures may require immobilisation, reduction, regular monitoring or surgical fixation depending on the type and location of the injury.
Joint Replacement Advanced arthritis or severe joint damage can sometimes significantly affect mobility and quality of life. In selected patients, procedures such as knee replacement or hip replacement may be considered after a detailed evaluation.
Surgical Treatment When conservative approaches are not sufficient or when a condition requires surgical intervention, an orthopaedic surgeon may recommend an appropriate procedure.
